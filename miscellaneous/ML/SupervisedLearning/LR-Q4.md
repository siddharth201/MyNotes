## Q. Why MSE + Logistic Regression becomes non-convex ?  

To understand why MSE + Logistic Regression becomes non-convex, let’s look at a simple example with just one data point.
Imagine we have a single training example where the true answer is $y = 1$.
In Logistic Regression, our prediction ($\hat{y}$) is generated by passing a weight ($w$) through the S-shaped sigmoid function:
$$\hat{y} = \frac{1}{1 + e^{-w}}$$ 
If we calculate the Mean Squared Error (MSE) for this single prediction, the loss equation is:
$$\text{Loss} = (1 - \hat{y})^2$$

### The Intuitive Reason It Fails: "The Plateau Effect"
A perfectly convex function must shape like a bowl where the slope gets steeper the further away you are from the bottom. But notice the left side of the graph above:

   1. The Core Issue: When your weight ($w$) is highly negative (e.g., $-5$), the model is confidently wrong. It predicts $\hat{y} \approx 0$, even though the real answer is $1$.
   2. The Consequence: Because of the flattening nature of the sigmoid curve at extreme ends, the slope (gradient) of the loss curve becomes completely flat on the far left.
   3. The Trap: If Gradient Descent starts on that far-left plateau, it looks around, sees a slope of almost zero, and assumes, "I must already be at the bottom!" It gets stuck in a flat region with a massive error rate.

### How Log-Loss Fixes This
Log-loss uses a logarithm ($-\ln(\hat{y})$) instead of a square power. The logarithm acts like a magnifying glass for catastrophic mistakes: as your prediction gets closer to $0$ when it should be $1$, the log-loss shoots up to infinity, creating a perfectly steep, continuous slope that guides Gradient Descent straight to the global minimum every single time.
