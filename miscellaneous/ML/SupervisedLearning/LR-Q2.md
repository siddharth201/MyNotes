Here is a straightforward breakdown of what the question is asking and how the code solves it.

## Part 1: Understanding the Question
The problem asks you to calculate the overall performance error (log-loss) of a logistic regression model based on two inputs:

   1. z (Logits): The raw numbers generated by the model before deciding a probability.
   2. y_true (Actual labels): The real answers ($1$ or $0$) for each data point.

The question gives you the standard formula for the error of a single observation:
$$\text{Error} = -y \log(\hat{y}) - (1 - y) \log(1 - \hat{y})$$ 
However, the formula needs $\hat{y}$ (the predicted probability), but you only have z. The question notes that $\hat{y}$ is predicted by applying the sigmoid function to $z$.
Your goal is to calculate the error for every item in the list, average them together, and round the final answer to two decimal places.

## Part 2: Understanding the Solution Step-by-Step
Because z and y_true are converted into NumPy arrays (np.asarray), we can perform math on the entire list all at once (called vectorization).
## Step 1: Turn raw scores into probabilities ($\hat{y}$)

probability = 1 / (1 + np.exp(-z))


* What it does: This is the mathematical Sigmoid Function. It squashes every raw value in the z array into a decimal between $0$ and $1$, representing the model's confidence level ($\hat{y}$).

## Step 2: Calculate the error for every data point

loss_array = - (y_true * np.log(probability) + (1 - y_true) * np.log(1 - probability))


* What it does: This applies the exact log-loss formula from the problem text to the entire array.
* How it works intuitively:
* If the true answer is $y = 1$, the right half of the formula $(1 - 1) \cdot \log(1 - \hat{y})$ becomes $0$. The formula simplifies to just $-\log(\text{probability})$.
   * If the true answer is $y = 0$, the left half $0 \cdot \log(\hat{y})$ becomes $0$. The formula simplifies to just $-\log(1 - \text{probability})$.

## Step 3: Average the errors together

loss = np.mean(loss_array)


* What it does: loss_array contains individual error scores for every single prediction. np.mean() adds all those errors up and divides by the total number of observations to find the model's overall average error.

## Step 4: Round the result

return np.round(loss, 2)


* What it does: Ensures the final answer strictly matches the output format required by the platform (e.g., transforming 1.32847... into 1.33).
