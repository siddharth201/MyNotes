
The range of the slope ($m$) is all real numbers, which can be written mathematically as:
$$\mathbf{-\infty < m < \infty} \quad \text{or} \quad \mathbf{m \in (-\infty, \infty)}$$ 

## 💡 What the Range Means Visually
Because the slope can be any number, a line can tilt in any direction:

* $m > 0$ (Positive): The line goes up from left to right. The larger the number, the steeper the climb.
* $m < 0$ (Negative): The line goes down from left to right.
* $m = 0$: The line is completely flat (horizontal), like the line $y = c$. This is the exact strategy used by the "Lazy Model" we discussed earlier!

## ⚠️ The One Exception: Vertical Lines
A perfectly vertical line has an undefined slope (or a slope that approaches infinity). However, a vertical line cannot be written in the form $y = mx + c$; its equation is always written as $x = \text{constant}$.
Therefore, for any line that can be written in the $y = mx + c$ format, the slope can be absolutely any real number.
