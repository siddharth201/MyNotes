
## Section: 1. Percentages, Ratios, Profit & Loss

---

**Question:** The cost of production of a cordless phone set in 2002 is Rs. 900, divided between material, labor, and overheads in the ratio 3:4:2. If it is marked to give a 20% profit on the labor component, what is the marked price?

**Formula:** $\text{Marked Price} = \text{Total Cost} \times \left(1 + \frac{r_{\text{target}}}{R_{\text{total}}} \times \frac{P}{100}\right)$

**Solution:** $\text{Labor Cost} = \frac{4}{9} \times 900 = \text{Rs. } 400$. Profit $= 20\% \text{ of } 400 = \text{Rs. } 80$. $\text{Marked Price} = 900 + 80 = \mathbf{\text{Rs. } 980/-}$.

**Question:** A trader charges 20% above the cost price. If he discounts 10% of the bill for cash payment, what is his gain?

**Formula:** $\text{Net Gain \%} = x - y - \frac{xy}{100}$

**Solution:** $20 - 10 - \frac{20 \times 10}{100} = 10 - 2 = \mathbf{8\%}$.

**Question:** The salary of Amit is 30% more than that of Varun. By what percentage is Varun's salary less than Amit's?

**Formula:** $\% \text{ Less} = \frac{R}{100 + R} \times 100$

**Solution:** $\frac{30}{100 + 30} \times 100 = \frac{300}{13} \approx \mathbf{23.07\%}$.

**Question:** Population of a village increased by 5% from 2007 to 2008 and by 25% from 2008 *(printed as 2005)* to 2009. If the population was 480 in 2007, what was it in 2009?

**Formula:** $\text{Final Value} = \text{Initial} \times \left(1 + \frac{x}{100}\right) \times \left(1 + \frac{y}{100}\right)$

**Solution:** $480 \times 1.05 \times 1.25 = 504 \times 1.25 = \mathbf{630}$.

**Question:** Hiten spent 45% of his March salary on bills and rent, invested 60% of the remainder in PPF, and deposited the remaining Rs. 15,400 in the bank. With a 10% increment in April, what was his April salary?

**Formula:** $\text{Remaining} = \text{Salary} \times \left(1 - \frac{a}{100}\right) \times \left(1 - \frac{b}{100}\right)$

**Solution:** Bank share $= 0.55 \times 0.40 = 22\%$. March Salary $= \frac{15,400}{0.22} = \text{Rs. } 70,000$. April Salary $= 70,000 \times 1.10 = \mathbf{\text{Rs. } 77,000}$.

**Question:** In a library, 60% of books are English, 30% Hindi, and 10% Tamil. If 40% of English, 50% of Hindi, and 20% of Tamil books are fiction, what percentage of non-fiction books are not in English?

**Formula:** $\text{Required \%} = \frac{\text{Non-English Non-Fiction}}{\text{Total Non-Fiction}} \times 100$

**Solution:** Assume 100 books. Non-fiction: English $= 36$, Hindi $= 15$, Tamil $= 8$ (Total $= 59$). Non-English non-fiction $= 15 + 8 = 23$. $\frac{23}{59} \times 100 \approx 38.98\% \approx \mathbf{40\%}$.

## Section: 2. Coding-Decoding

---

**Question:** If 0 is coded as `I` and 1 as `H` with place values doubling to the left, how is 20% of 45 written?

**Formula:** Convert decimal to binary using weights $8, 4, 2, 1$, then map $1 \to \text{H}$ and $0 \to \text{I}$.

**Solution:** $20\% \text{ of } 45 = 9 = 8 + 1 = 1001_2 \to \mathbf{\text{H I I H}}$.

**Question:** If `CLOUD` is coded as `BKNTC`, how is `SHINE` coded?

**Formula:** $\text{Coded Letter} = \text{Original Position} - 1$

**Solution:** `S→R`, `H→G`, `I→H`, `N→M`, `E→D` $\to \mathbf{\text{RGHMD}}$.

**Question:** If `CAT` is coded as `3120`, how is `DOG` coded?

**Formula:** Direct alphabetical position (`A=1` to `Z=26`).

**Solution:** `D=4`, `O=15`, `G=7` $\to \mathbf{4157}$.

**Question:** If `DOG` is `4157` and `CAT` is `6120`, how is `BAD` coded?

**Formula:** Direct alphabetical position (`A=1` to `Z=26`).

**Solution:** `B=2`, `A=1`, `D=4` $\to \mathbf{214}$.

**Question:** If `BACK` is coded as `21311`, how is `ENGAGE` coded?

**Formula:** Direct alphabetical position (`A=1` to `Z=26`).

**Solution:** `E=5`, `N=14`, `G=7`, `A=1`, `G=7`, `E=5` $\to \mathbf{5147175}$.

**Question:** If `OFFICE` is `422168` and `CEREAL` is `683857`, how is `CLAIR` coded?

**Formula:** 1-to-1 direct letter-to-digit substitution.

**Solution:** `C=6`, `L=7`, `A=5`, `I=1`, `R=3` $\to \mathbf{67513}$.

**Question:** If `HOUSE` is `25310` and `LEASING` is `9061784`, how is `GEASE` coded?

**Formula:** 1-to-1 direct letter-to-digit substitution.

**Solution:** `G=4`, `E=0`, `A=6`, `S=1`, `E=0` $\to \mathbf{40610}$.

**Question:** If `TEN` is `256` and `SIXTY` is `19827`, how is `SIXTEEN` coded?

**Formula:** 1-to-1 direct letter-to-digit substitution.

**Solution:** `S=1`, `I=9`, `X=8`, `T=2`, `E=5`, `E=5`, `N=6` $\to \mathbf{1982556}$.

## Section: 3. Non-Verbal Reasoning — Visual Matrices

---

**Question:** Complete the 2×2 spiral pattern (Q#4).

**Formula:** Element Count Rule: $+2$ line segments moving left to right.

**Solution:** Row 2 adds 2 segments to the 6-segment spiral, forming an 8-segment open square $\to \mathbf{C}$.

**Question:** Complete the 2×2 3D cube perspective matrix (Q#8).

**Formula:** Perspective Shift Rule: Top-down view shifts to bottom-up view.

**Solution:** Applying the bottom-up shift to Row 2 while preserving the `+` and dot faces $\to \mathbf{A}$.

**Question:** Complete the 3×3 shaded corners & arrow grid (Q#10).

**Formula:** Column Matching Rule: Col 3 shading $=$ Col 2 shading; Col 3 arrow $=$ Col 1 arrow.

**Solution:** Bottom-right cross-hatched square + upward arrow (`↑`) $\to \mathbf{F}$.

**Question:** Complete the 2×2 motif rotation pattern (Q#13).

**Formula:** Rotation Rule: $90^\circ$ clockwise rotation left to right.

**Solution:** Rotating the left-pointing motif $90^\circ$ clockwise makes it point up with the forked tail at the bottom $\to \mathbf{C}$.

**Question:** Complete the 3×3 arrow grid (Q#16).

**Formula:** Multi-attribute Rule: Col 3 points left; fill alternates W/B/W; Row 3 stays centered.

**Solution:** White, centered, left-pointing arrow $\to \mathbf{E}$.

**Question:** Complete the 3×3 symbol perimeter matrix (Q#19).

**Formula:** Independent Tracking: Circle alternates B/W/B; Star moves 1 step clockwise; Diamond alternates TL $\leftrightarrow$ BR.

**Solution:** Black circle (mid-right), star (top-center), white diamond (top-left) $\to \mathbf{D}$.

**Question:** Complete the 2×2 geometric shapes matrix (Q#20).

**Formula:** Set Conservation Rule: Exact same 4 shapes (2 black, 2 white) are preserved.

**Solution:** Only option preserving the black right-triangle, small black triangle, white diamond, and white pentagon $\to \mathbf{E}$.

**Question:** Complete the 2×2 nested shapes matrix (Q#23).

**Formula:** Subtraction Rule: Remove the innermost filled element left to right.

**Solution:** Removing the inner hatched square from the black frame leaves a black frame with a white center $\to \mathbf{B}$.

**Question:** Complete the 2×2 grid shift matrix (Q#26).

**Formula:** Translation Rule: Shift all elements 1 column to the left.

**Solution:** Black L-shape moves to columns 1–2 (top-left) and bottom star moves to column 2 $\to \mathbf{E}$.

**Question:** Complete the 3×3 spoke-and-dot matrix (Q#29).

**Formula:** Rotation Rule: N/S dots stay black; the other opposite black pair rotates $45^\circ$ counter-clockwise.

**Solution:** N/S black + SW/NE black $\to \mathbf{C}$.

## Section: 4. Verbal & Clerical Comparison ("Same or Different")

---

**Question:** Compare: `a Zebra` and `a Horse`

**Formula:** Conceptual Equivalence Check.

**Solution:** Two distinct species $\to \mathbf{\text{Different}}$.

**Question:** Compare: `United States of America` :: `USA`

**Formula:** Conceptual Equivalence Check.

**Solution:** Standard abbreviation for the same country $\to \mathbf{\text{Same}}$.

**Question:** Compare: `The capital of France` :: `Paris`

**Formula:** Conceptual Equivalence Check.

**Solution:** Both refer to the exact same city $\to \mathbf{\text{Same}}$.

**Question:** Compare: `The Quick Brown Fox Jumps Over The Lazy Fox.` :: `The Quick Brown Fox Jumps Over The Lazy Dog.`

**Formula:** Character-by-character string check.

**Solution:** Ends with `Fox.` vs. `Dog.` $\to \mathbf{\text{Different}}$.

**Question:** Compare: `Johnson & Johnson` :: `Johnson & Johnsons`

**Formula:** Character-by-character string check.

**Solution:** Extra `s` at the end of the second string $\to \mathbf{\text{Different}}$.

**Question:** Compare: `UNIX-to-UNIX Network` :: `UNIX-To-UNIX Network`

**Formula:** Case-sensitive string check.

**Solution:** Lowercase `to` vs. capitalized `To` $\to \mathbf{\text{Different}}$.

**Question:** Compare: `Frankfurt Hoechst` :: `Frankfurt Hoechst`

**Formula:** Character-by-character string check.

**Solution:** Exact character match $\to \mathbf{\text{Same}}$.

**Question:** Compare: `Campagnolo` :: `Campagnalo`

**Formula:** Character-by-character string check.

**Solution:** 8th letter differs (`o` vs. `a`) $\to \mathbf{\text{Different}}$.

**Question:** Compare: `Blaupunkt & Decker` :: `Blaupukt & Decker`

**Formula:** Character-by-character string check.

**Solution:** Missing `n` in `Blaupukt` $\to \mathbf{\text{Different}}$.

## Section: 5. Data Sufficiency

---

**Question:** How many children are in the group? I. Sangita scored more than 12 children. II. Reena scored less than Sangita.

**Formula:** $\text{Total} = \text{Rank from Top} + \text{Rank from Bottom} - 1$

**Solution:** Neither statement gives Sangita's rank from the top $\to \mathbf{\text{Statements I and II together are not sufficient}}$.

**Question:** What is the value of $X$? I. $A\#B$ means $A - B$. II. $A*B$ means $A + B$.

**Formula:** Variable Presence Check.

**Solution:** Neither statement mentions $X$ or provides an equation $\to \mathbf{\text{Statements I and II together are not sufficient}}$.

**Question:** What is the price of one pen? I. 5 pens + 3 pencils = Rs. 40. II. 10 pens + 6 pencils = Rs. 80.

**Formula:** Independent Equations Check: $\frac{a_1}{a_2} \neq \frac{b_1}{b_2}$.

**Solution:** Statement II is simply $2 \times$ Statement I (dependent equations) $\to \mathbf{\text{Statements I and II together are not sufficient}}$.

**Question:** What is the code for `'fat'`? I. `'she is fat'` = `'he ra ca'`. II. `'fat boy'` = `'ra ka'`.

**Formula:** Set Intersection Rule (Common Word $=$ Common Code).

**Solution:** Combining I & II isolates `'fat'` $= \text{'ra'}$ $\to \mathbf{\text{Statements I and II together are sufficient}}$.

**Question:** Who among $L, N, F, G, Q$ reached college first? I. $F$ before $L, G$ but not before $Q$, who was not first. II. $N$ before $F, G$ and $L$ after $F$.

**Formula:** Elimination Rule ($n - 1$ eliminated $\implies$ remaining 1 is unique).

**Solution:** Statement I alone eliminates $L, G, F,$ and $Q$, leaving $N$ as first $\to \mathbf{\text{Statement I alone is sufficient}}$.

## Section: 6. Time, Speed, Distance, Trains & Boats/Streams

---

**Question:** A person covers a distance at 120 km/h in 90 minutes. At what speed must he travel to cover it in 1 hour 20 minutes?

**Formula:** $S_1 \times T_1 = S_2 \times T_2$

**Solution:** $120 \times 90 = S_2 \times 80 \implies S_2 = \frac{10800}{80} = \mathbf{135\text{ km/h}}$.

**Question:** A train running at 60 km/hr crosses a pole in 9 seconds. What is the length of the train?

**Formula:** $\text{Length} = \left(\text{Speed in km/hr} \times \frac{5}{18}\right) \times \text{Time}$

**Solution:** $60 \times \frac{5}{18} \times 9 = \mathbf{150\text{ m}}$.

**Question:** A boat with still-water speed 15 km/hr goes 30 km downstream and returns in 4 hours 30 minutes. Find the speed of the stream.

**Formula:** $T = \frac{2du}{u^2 - v^2}$

**Solution:** $\frac{9}{2} = \frac{2 \times 30 \times 15}{225 - v^2} \implies 225 - v^2 = 200 \implies v^2 = 25 \implies v = \mathbf{5\text{ km/hr}}$.

**Question:** A boat travels 30 km upstream and 44 km downstream in 10 hrs; 40 km upstream and 55 km downstream in 13 hrs. Find the still-water speed.

**Formula:** $\frac{d_1}{U} + \frac{d_2}{D} = T$, and $u = \frac{D + U}{2}$

**Solution:** Solving gives $D = 11\text{ km/hr}$ and $U = 5\text{ km/hr}$. $u = \frac{11 + 5}{2} = \mathbf{8\text{ km/hr}}$ *(exact value; options misprinted)*.

## Section: 7. Simple Interest

---

**Question:** A principal of Rs. 4000 is invested at 6% p.a. simple interest for 3 years. Calculate the total interest earned.

**Formula:** $\text{SI} = \frac{P \times R \times T}{100}$

**Solution:** $\frac{4000 \times 6 \times 3}{100} = \mathbf{\text{Rs. } 720}$.

**Question:** A man invested $2000 at 10% p.a. SI for 2 years, then reinvested the principal and interest for 1 year at 8% p.a. SI. Find the final amount.

**Formula:** $P_2 = P_1 + \text{SI}_1$, then $\text{Final Amount} = P_2 + \frac{P_2 \times R_2 \times T_2}{100}$

**Solution:** $\text{SI}_1 = 400 \implies P_2 = 2400$. $\text{SI}_2 = \frac{2400 \times 8 \times 1}{100} = 192$. Final Amount $= 2400 + 192 = \mathbf{\$2592}$ *(closest option: $2600)*.

## Section: 8. Problems on Ages & Averages

---

**Question:** Six years ago, the ratio of Kunal's and Sagar's ages was 6:5. Four years hence, it will be 11:10. Find Sagar's present age.

**Formula:** Ratio-Difference Shortcut: $\Delta\text{ ratio units} = \Delta\text{ years}$ (when ratio differences match).

**Solution:** $5\text{ units} = 10\text{ years} \implies 1\text{ unit} = 2\text{ years}$. Sagar's age 6 years ago $= 5 \times 2 = 10 \implies$ Present age $= 10 + 6 = \mathbf{16\text{ years}}$.

**Question:** Ages of three siblings are in the ratio 3:4:5. Five years ago, the sum of their ages was 39. Find the current age of the oldest sibling.

**Formula:** $\text{Present Sum} = \text{Past Sum} + (n \times t)$

**Solution:** Present Sum $= 39 + (3 \times 5) = 54$. $1\text{ part} = \frac{54}{12} = 4.5$. Oldest $= 5 \times 4.5 = \mathbf{22.5}$ *(none of the listed options)*.

**Question:** The average age of 12 students is 20 years. When 4 more join, the average increases by 1 year. Find the average age of the 4 new students.

**Formula:** $\text{Avg}_{\text{new}} = \frac{(N_{\text{total}} \times \text{Avg}_{\text{final}}) - (N_{\text{old}} \times \text{Avg}_{\text{old}})}{N_{\text{new}}}$

**Solution:** $\frac{(16 \times 21) - (12 \times 20)}{4} = \frac{336 - 240}{4} = \frac{96}{4} = \mathbf{24}$.

## Section: 9. Simplification & Arithmetic

---

**Question:** $24\% \text{ of } 150 \times \frac{3}{4} = ?$

**Formula:** `BODMAS` and $x\% \text{ of } y = \frac{x}{100} \times y$

**Solution:** $\left(\frac{24}{100} \times 150\right) \times \frac{3}{4} = 36 \times \frac{3}{4} = \mathbf{27}$ *(none of the listed options; evaluates to 48 if $\div \frac{3}{4}$)*.

**Question:** $\frac{3}{17} \text{ of } 20\% \text{ of } 510 + 7 = x$

**Formula:** `BODMAS`

**Solution:** $\left(\frac{3}{17} \times \frac{1}{5} \times 510\right) + 7 = (3 \times 6) + 7 = 18 + 7 = \mathbf{25}$.

## Section: 10. Standalone Aptitude & Reasoning

---

**Question:** If 1st August 2010 is Sunday, what day will be on 1st August 2012?

**Formula:** Ordinary year $= +1$ odd day; Leap year (crossing Feb 29) $= +2$ odd days.

**Solution:** $2010 \to 2011$ ($+1$) and $2011 \to 2012$ ($+2$, leap year). Total $= +3$ days from Sunday $\to \mathbf{\text{Wednesday}}$.

**Question:** $A$ finishes work in 15 days and $B$ in 20 days. If they work together for 4 days, what fraction of work is left?

**Formula:** $\text{Work Left} = 1 - d\left(\frac{1}{A} + \frac{1}{B}\right)$

**Solution:** $1 - 4\left(\frac{1}{15} + \frac{1}{20}\right) = 1 - 4\left(\frac{7}{60}\right) = 1 - \frac{7}{15} = \mathbf{\frac{8}{15}}$.

**Question:** A card is drawn from a pack of 52 cards. Find the probability of getting a queen of club or a king of heart.

**Formula:** $P(A \text{ or } B) = P(A) + P(B)$

**Solution:** $\frac{1}{52} + \frac{1}{52} = \frac{2}{52} = \mathbf{\frac{1}{26}}$.

**Question:** Find the odd one out: `8, 27, 64, 125, 216`.

**Formula:** Perfect Cube ($n^3$) vs. Perfect Square ($n^2$) test.

**Solution:** All are cubes ($2^3$ to $6^3$), but **64** is the only number that is both a perfect cube ($4^3$) and a perfect square ($8^2$) $\to \mathbf{64}$.
