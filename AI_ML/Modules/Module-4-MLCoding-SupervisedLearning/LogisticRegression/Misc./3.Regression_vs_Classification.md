The easiest way to identify if a problem belongs to Regression or Classification is to look closely at the Target Variable (the label/output you want to predict). You don't need to look at the inputs or features—only the final answer you are trying to guess.
Here is the golden rule for telling them apart:

* Classification is about predicting a discrete label, category, or class. You are choosing from a finite set of options.
* Regression is about predicting a continuous numerical value. You are calculating a precise number on an infinite scale.

------------------------------
## The Quick Identity Checklist
Ask yourself these three questions about your target variable to instantly know which type of problem you are facing:

| Question | If the answer is... ➡️ | It is a Classification Problem | If the answer is... ➡️ | It is a Regression Problem |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| What type of data is the output? | Qualitative / Categorical | Words, text labels, or distinct status codes (e.g., Passed, Failed). | Quantitative / Continuous | Real numbers, floats, or integers where the exact math matters (e.g., 14.50, $250,000). |
| How many possible answers exist? | Finite & Countable | A fixed list of choices (e.g., 2 choices for Spam/Not Spam, 4 choices for Blood Type). | Infinite | The answer could be literally any number within a range (e.g., temperature could be 22.4°C, 22.41°C, etc.). |
| Does "between" make sense? | No | An output cannot be halfway between "Cat" and "Dog." | Yes | An output can be halfway between $50 and $60 ($55). |

------------------------------
## Side-by-Side Comparison of Common Use Cases
Often, the exact same business goal can be framed as either classification or regression, depending on how you want the final answer formatted.

| The Goal | Framed as Classification (Predicting a Tag) | Framed as Regression (Predicting a Number) |
|---|---|---|
| Weather | Will it be Sunny, Rainy, or Cloudy tomorrow? | What will the exact temperature be tomorrow in Celsius? |
| Real Estate | Is this house Overpriced, Fairly Priced, or a Bargain? | What is the exact market value ($) of this house? |
| E-Commerce | Will this customer buy this item or not? | How much money ($) will this customer spend on our website this month? |
| Healthcare | Does the patient have diabetes? (Yes / No) | What will the patient's blood sugar level be after eating? |

## A Common Trap: Numbers that are actually Categories
Be careful when your data uses numbers as labels. If your model predicts a user's Zip Code (e.g., 90210) or Star Rating (1, 2, 3, 4, 5), this is still Classification.
Even though they look like numbers, a zip code is just a category for a location, and a 4-star rating is a discrete bucket. You wouldn't average two zip codes together to find a middle location!
