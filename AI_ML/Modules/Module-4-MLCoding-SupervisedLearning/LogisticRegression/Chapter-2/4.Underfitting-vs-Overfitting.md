# Overfitting vs. Underfitting

Overfitting and underfitting are the two fundamental failure modes in machine learning describing how well a model generalizes from training data to new, unseen data.

---

### 1. Underfitting (High Bias)
Underfitting occurs when a model is too simple to capture the underlying patterns in the dataset. It fails to learn even from the training data.

* Analogy: A student who only memorized one formula and tries to use it for every single exam question.
* Symptoms: Poor performance on the training set AND poor performance on the validation/test set.
* Common Causes:
  - Using a linear model for inherently non-linear relationships.
  - Insufficient features or over-simplifying feature representations.
  - Over-regularization (penalties are set too high).
* Fixes:
  - Increase model complexity (e.g., deeper trees, polynomial features, neural networks).
  - Engineer or add more informative features.
  - Reduce regularization constraints.

---

### 2. Overfitting (High Variance)
Overfitting occurs when a model is too complex and memorizes the training data, including its random noise and outliers, rather than learning the actual underlying signal.

* Analogy: A student who memorized the exact answers and typos of a practice exam by heart, but fails when the actual exam changes the numbers.
* Symptoms: Extremely high accuracy on the training set, but poor accuracy on the validation/test set.
* Common Causes:
  - Model has too many parameters relative to the number of data points (e.g., an unconstrained Decision Tree).
  - Training data is small, noisy, or unrepresentative.
* Fixes:
  - Regularization: Add L1 (Lasso) or L2 (Ridge) penalties.
  - Cross-Validation: Use k-fold cross-validation to ensure reliable validation signals.
  - Pruning / Constraints: Limit tree depth (max_depth), increase minimum samples per leaf.
  - More Data: Collect more samples or apply data augmentation.
  - Feature Selection: Remove noisy, redundant, or irrelevant features.

---

### Side-by-Side Comparison

| Property | Underfitting | Optimal Fit | Overfitting |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Statistical Term | High Bias | Balanced | High Variance |
| Model Complexity | Too simple | Just right | Too complex |
| Training Error | High | Low | Extremely Low / Near zero |
| Validation Error | High | Low | High |
| Generalization | Fails everywhere | Generalizes well | Fails on new data |
