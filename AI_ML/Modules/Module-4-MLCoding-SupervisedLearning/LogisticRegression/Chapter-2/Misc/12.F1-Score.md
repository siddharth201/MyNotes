# F1 Score — Simple Explanation

## 1. First, remember Precision and Recall

You already learned:

### Precision

> **When the model predicts Positive, how often is it correct?**

`Precision = TP / (TP + FP)`

### Recall

> **Out of all actual Positive cases, how many did the model find?**

`Recall = TP / (TP + FN)`

The problem is:

> One model can have high Precision but low Recall, while another can have low Precision but high Recall.

So how do we compare them with **one number**?

That's where **F1 Score** comes in.

---

# 2. What is F1 Score?

F1 Score combines:

- Precision
- Recall

into a **single metric**.

The formula is:

$F1 = \frac{2 \times Precision \times Recall}{Precision + Recall}$$

It is the **harmonic mean** of Precision and Recall.

The important word is **harmonic mean**.

---

# 3. Why not simply take the average?

Suppose:

**Precision = 0.9**

**Recall = 0.1**

Arithmetic average would be:

$$\frac{0.9+0.1}{2}=0.5$$

So we might think:

> "The model has 50% performance."

But that's misleading because Recall is extremely poor.

F1 Score gives more importance to the fact that **one metric is very low**.

Calculate:

$$F1 = \frac{2(0.9)(0.1)}{0.9+0.1}$$

$$F1=0.18$$

So:

**F1 = 0.18**

That's much lower than the arithmetic average of 0.5.

### This is the key intuition:

> **F1 Score becomes low when either Precision or Recall is low.**

Think about three situations.

### Situation 1

Precision = 0.8  
Recall = 0.8

Both are good.

→ **F1 will be high**

---

### Situation 2

Precision = 0.9

One is excellent, one is terrible.

→ **F1 will be low**

---

### Situation 3

Precision = 0.6  
Recall = 0.6

Both are moderate.

→ **F1 will be around 0.6**

So:

> **F1 rewards balance.**

# 10. Why is it called Harmonic Mean?

You don't need to go deep into mathematical theory for your current ML preparation.

Just remember:

For two numbers:

**Arithmetic Mean**

`(P + R) / 2`

**Harmonic Mean**

`2PR / (P + R)`

F1 uses the **harmonic mean** because we don't want a model to hide a very poor Precision or Recall behind a high value of the other.

---

# 11. A very important property

If:

**Precision = 1**

and:

**Recall = 0**

then:

**F1 = 0**

If:

**Precision = 0**

and:

**Recall = 1**

then:

**F1 = 0**

So:

> **If either Precision or Recall is zero, F1 becomes zero.**

This reinforces the idea that F1 requires both.

---

# 12. When should we use F1 Score?

Use F1 when:

> **Both False Positives and False Negatives matter, and you want a balance between Precision and Recall.**

For example:

### Spam detection

You don't want:

- too many legitimate emails marked spam → FP
- too much spam reaching the user → FN

So balancing Precision and Recall can be useful.

### Information retrieval

You want to find relevant results but don't want too many irrelevant results.

### F1 Score

> **How well do I balance Precision and Recall?**

---

# 14. One important caution

Don't memorize:

> "Always use F1."

That's incorrect.

If **False Negatives are much more dangerous**, like certain medical screening situations, you may prioritize **Recall**.

If **False Positives are much more costly**, you may prioritize **Precision**.

If both matter reasonably equally:

> **F1 Score is a useful choice.**

This connects directly with what you learned in the previous video.
